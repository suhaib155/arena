Protocol & contracts

Eight contracts, deployed to Base Sepolia on 27 May 2026. They are not connected to the app, and nothing has been minted on them.

The protocol at a glance

The Postgres database behind the app is the system of record today. It holds who covered which zones, the state of every battle, and every balance. These contracts are the design for moving that onchain, and until they are wired up and deployed to Base mainnet they describe an intention rather than a system.

The deployment is on Base Sepolia, chain 84532. Nothing is on Base mainnet. One account holds DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE on all eight contracts, and that same account is also the oracle operator, the keeper, and the treasury address.

The contracts

The eight deployed contracts and what each is for
ContractRole
$MOVE tokenThe network's asset. Designed to be minted by verified movement within a capped schedule, then spent and burned across the game. No supply has been minted.
Zone DeedsThe ownership primitive. One deed per map zone, held under the owner's keys, carrying provenance, yield, and defensive strength. No deed has been minted.
GearA second NFT contract for in-game gear, minted against $MOVE.
ChallengeThe contest system. A challenger stakes to compete for a held zone, scores are submitted, and the deed holds or changes hands.
Season controllerRuns 90-day seasons, pauses minting before a season ends, and adjusts the emission rate afterwards.
VaultStaking and the treasury balance the network is designed to draw on.
DAOOnchain proposals and voting over protocol parameters and the treasury. Not in use.
Movement oracleHolds the operator key that signs movement attestations, so the contracts above can act on activity they did not observe.

How challenge settlement actually works

It is worth being precise about this one, because it is easy to describe as more autonomous than it is. A challenge resolves when anyone calls resolveChallenge after the window closes. The contract compares the challenger's score against the defender's, adjusted for loyalty and any stronghold boost, and on a challenger win it calls safeTransferFrom to move the deed.

Two things make that less trustless than it sounds. The transfer only succeeds if the losing defender had already granted the challenge contract approval over their deeds, so a defender who never approves cannot lose one. And the scores are not derived from movement by the contract; they arrive through submitScore backed by a signature from the oracle operator. Settlement therefore depends on a key the team holds. Both are known gaps rather than the intended end state.

Design intent, and where it is not met

  • Trust-minimized. The contracts act on signed attestations rather than raw client input. The signer is a single operator key.
  • Role-scoped. Privileged actions are gated by named roles. On this deployment one account holds all of them, so the separation exists in the code and not in practice.
  • Timelocked. Not yet. A timelock has been written but no timelock contract is deployed, so an admin change takes effect the moment it is sent.
  • Standards-based. Built on OpenZeppelin's AccessControl, ERC-20, and ERC-721 implementations rather than hand-rolled primitives.

Asset design is in the $MOVE economy, ownership mechanics in Zone Deeds, and the road to community control in Governance.
