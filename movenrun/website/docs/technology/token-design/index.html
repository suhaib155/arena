$MOVE economy

$MOVE is designed to be minted by movement, demanded by utility, and tightened by sinks. The token contract is on Base Sepolia and no supply has been minted, so this page is a design document.

This describes the $MOVE economic design. It is not financial advice, an offer, or a promise of returns, and it quotes no price or yield figure. Parameters would be set through governance and may change. See Legal & disclaimers.

The economic loop

How $MOVE would enter

$MOVE is minted by verified movement, never by a faucet. Emission follows a capped, decaying schedule, highest early and stepping down over time, so the network can bootstrap without inflating forever. A daily cap per address prevents one account from dominating issuance, and it halves each epoch alongside the emission rate.

Where demand would come from

Demand is meant to come from things players want to do, rather than from speculation:

  • Territory. Claiming zones, upgrading, and fortifying cost $MOVE.
  • Competition. Declaring a challenge and entering seasonal contests cost $MOVE.
  • Staking. $MOVE can be staked in the vault.
  • Governance. Holding and staking gives a say in how parameters are set.

Sinks and balance

Several actions burn $MOVE outright, removing it from supply, so that activity tightens the float rather than diluting it. Minting a Zone Deed, declaring a challenge, boosting a stronghold, extending a challenge window, and minting gear all burn. A 2% zone tax on minted $MOVE routes value to the deed owner of the hex it came from, weighted by how long they have held it.

One thing carries a misleading name. The season-end Great Burn takes 10% of the top zones' accumulated yield and transfers it to the treasury. Total supply does not change, so it is a treasury transfer rather than a burn, and it is tracked as something to fix rather than something to describe as a sink.

Ownership on top

Zone Deeds are the ownership layer above the token. Active holders would accrue standing and in-game yield, which ties long-term participation to long-term ownership. No deed exists, so no yield has accrued to anyone.

Economic principles

  • Minted by checked activity, never by unverified claims.
  • Capped and decaying issuance, with a per-address daily cap.
  • Sinks and caps designed before incentives.
  • Parameters set through governance once governance exists. Today one key sets them.
