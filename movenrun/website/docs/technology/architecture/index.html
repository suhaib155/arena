Architecture

Three layers. The app and the verification server run today, and the protocol layer is deployed to a test network and not connected to either.

The three layers

What each layer does, and whether it runs
LayerRole
ExperienceThe mobile app: onboarding, movement sessions, the territory map, clubs, and the game. Live, and running on the device. There is no wallet in it.
VerificationTurns a recorded route into a validated, deterministic proof of movement and writes the resulting zones to Postgres. Built. The app does not submit movement to it yet.
ProtocolEight contracts on Base Sepolia: $MOVE, the oracle, Zone Deeds, gear, challenges, seasons, the vault, and the DAO. Deployed, unused, and not called by the backend.

How activity becomes ownership

  1. A player moves, and the app records and scores the route on the device.
  2. The verification layer validates a submitted route, produces a deterministic proof, and records the zones covered. It is built, and the app is not yet wired to it. This is where the pipeline ends.
  3. Planned: the oracle attests that proof onchain.
  4. Planned: the contracts settle the result, capturing a zone, minting $MOVE, or updating a deed, under the player's own keys.

Design principles

  • Onchain source of truth. The goal. Today the database is the source of truth and the team can rewrite it, which is exactly what steps three and four are meant to fix.
  • Trust-minimized inputs. The contracts are written to act on signed attestations rather than raw client input. The signer is one operator key.
  • Low-friction UX. Embedded wallets and sponsored gas are the plan. Neither is built, and no wallet provider has been chosen.
  • Built for Base. Low fees, fast confirmation, and consumer-grade tooling, on Base Sepolia for now.
