Onchain

MovenRun's contracts run on Base Sepolia while the economy is tested. Mainnet at launch.

What follows is the design those contracts implement.

Deeds

One deed per cell. A deed is held in the player's own wallet, carries the cell's provenance and defensive strength, and can be transferred directly. Holding it is what entitles you to the tolls the cell earns.

Deeds are issued against ground already held in the game, so the supply of them is bounded by the map rather than by demand. A cell that goes dormant long enough is reclaimed and reopened, which keeps abandoned ground in circulation instead of permanently locked up by somebody who stopped playing.

Tolls

Every time $MOVE is issued for movement through a cell, a share of it goes to that cell's deed holder rather than to the mover. That is the toll, and it is what turns a held cell from a badge into a small piece of infrastructure.

Length of tenure improves the rate the holder keeps. A cell held past thirty days earns more than one taken last week, and the rate keeps improving through ninety and a hundred and eighty days. Long, consistent holding is the behaviour the economy pays for. That is deliberate, because it is the same behaviour that makes somebody healthier.

Holders can withdraw accumulated tolls at any time.

$MOVE and the supply schedule

$MOVE is capped at one billion tokens. Nothing mints outside the schedule, and there is no faucet.

Issuance is per kilometre of verified movement and halves roughly every six months. The first epoch pays ten $MOVE per kilometre against a daily ceiling of two hundred per account, and both numbers halve together at each step. The per account cap is what stops one very determined person absorbing an epoch.

A weekly valve sits on top. If the rate at which $MOVE is being consumed falls too far behind the rate it is issued, the base rate steps down. The valve only ever reduces.

Where it goes

Demand comes from things players want to do. Claiming a deed costs $MOVE, and the price scales with how many people are moving that week. Declaring a challenge costs $MOVE. So does fortifying a cell under pressure, extending a challenge window, and minting gear. Those costs are destroyed rather than recycled, so activity tightens supply instead of circulating it.

The order matters. Sinks and caps were designed before the incentives, because the previous generation of movement economies did it the other way round and did not survive it.

Governance

Parameters are meant to end up in the hands of the people playing, through onchain proposals and voting over the treasury and the emission settings. That handover is a later phase, and the roadmap says where it sits.
