MovenRun Whitepaper

A movement network, building toward Base · MovenRun documentation

This whitepaper describes the MovenRun protocol, product, and economy as designed. Most of the onchain half is not built: the contracts named here are deployed to Base Sepolia, Base's test network, and nothing has been minted on them. MovenRun is a development-stage product, this document is forward-looking, and the design may change. Nothing here is financial advice or a promise of returns. See Legal & disclaimers.

1. Introduction

MovenRun is a movement game that turns real-world routes into territory on a global hexagonal map. Players move through their city and claim the ground they cover. The design calls for that territory to be recorded onchain as an asset the player owns, defends, and can transfer, funded by a native token called $MOVE and eventually governed by the people who play. The app and the verification behind it exist today. The onchain layer does not, and this document is careful to say which is which.

2. The problem

Human movement is enormous and constant, and it benefits the mover almost not at all. Fitness apps turn it into private statistics. Location platforms turn it into data they sell. The first wave of move-to-earn turned it into token emissions with no underlying demand, and those economies came apart the moment sentiment shifted. Three failures recur. Progress is not ownable. Activity cannot be trusted. Onboarding filters out everyone who is not already crypto-native. MovenRun is an attempt at all three.

3. Product thesis

A number in a fitness app resets. Territory somebody else can take off you does not, and defending it is a reason to go out again on a day you would rather not. Making the map ownable, and backing every claim with a check that the movement happened, gives everyday activity a reason to matter that does not depend on a token price. Three principles follow:

  • Own the map. Territory should be an asset, not a scoreboard entry.
  • Prove the movement. Every claim is checked, so effort wins and a spoofing app does not.
  • Hide the plumbing. Onchain ownership with no onchain friction, or the audience stays small.

4. User experience

Onboarding is a single tap and the player is moving within seconds. A session records the route with live distance, time, and pace kept deliberately glanceable, so attention stays on the road. Finishing produces a summary and a capture moment that claims the territory. Around that core sit the systems that give it depth: saved routes, streaks, seasons, districts, collections, and clubs. 5. The territory protocol

The world is modelled as a hexagonal grid using the open H3 system, so every point on Earth belongs to exactly one zone with a stable identifier. A route becomes the ordered set of zones it passed through. Zones have a lifecycle, from unexplored to captured, held, contested, and fortified, and they aggregate into districts and cities. Holding, defending, and upgrading territory are the central verbs of the game. Each is designed to map to an onchain action, and each currently resolves against MovenRun's database instead. See Territory computation and Territory & ownership.

6. Proof of movement

An ownership economy is worth no more than its inputs, so this is the layer that got built first. Each session is validated for physical plausibility, meaning speed, accuracy, and continuity, then reduced to a deterministic route proof. Duplicate and replayed routes are rejected. That pipeline is built and runs in the backend. The app scores sessions on the device for now and reaches the server only to sign in. The next step, having the oracle attest that proof so contracts can act on it without trusting the client, is designed and not yet wired up. See Movement verification.

7. Onchain architecture

MovenRun is built for Base, an Ethereum Layer 2 chosen for low fees, fast confirmation, and consumer-grade tooling. The protocol is eight contracts: the $MOVE token, the movement oracle, the Zone Deed and gear NFTs, a challenge system for contesting land, a season controller, a staking vault, and the DAO. They were deployed to Base Sepolia, chain 84532, on 27 May 2026. Nothing is on Base mainnet.

The design goal is that the application layer never holds custody of ownership. It holds all of it today. Zones, battles, and balances live in a Postgres database the team operates, and the contracts on the test network have never been used. See Architecture and Protocol & contracts.

8. Making onchain invisible

Reach depends on removing every point of crypto friction, and this is the part of the plan that is furthest behind. The design calls for embedded wallets provisioned automatically, gas sponsored through account abstraction, and complex actions batched behind ordinary game buttons, with self-custody available to anyone who wants it. None of that exists. No embedded wallet provider has been selected, and the codebase contains no paymaster and no account-abstraction code. The interfaces are in place and every provider surface fails closed, which is a starting point rather than a feature. See Onchain, made invisible.

9. The $MOVE economy

$MOVE is designed as the asset that powers MovenRun. Verified movement mints it within a capped, decaying schedule, utility across the game creates demand, and several core actions burn it. Zone Deeds sit on top as the ownership layer, accruing standing and in-game yield to active holders. Sinks and caps were designed before incentives, which is the order the previous generation of these economies got wrong. No $MOVE has been minted and nobody has accrued yield. See the $MOVE economy.

10. Ownership: Zone Deeds

A Zone Deed is the onchain representation of a captured zone, one deed per map cell, held under the owner's keys. Deeds are designed to be transferable, to carry provenance, to accrue in-game yield and defensive strength while actively held, and to be challengeable by other players. The contract is deployed to Base Sepolia. No deed has been minted on it, so nothing described in this section has happened. See Zone Deeds.

11. Governance

MovenRun is designed to become community-owned, through onchain proposals, snapshot-based voting, and a timelock between a vote passing and taking effect. The starting point is a long way from that. One account holds DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE on all eight testnet contracts, and the same account is the oracle operator, the keeper, and the treasury. A timelock has been written but is not deployed, so nothing today imposes a delay on anything. See Governance & the DAO.

12. Roadmap

MovenRun advances in phases. A movement and capture beta that proves the loop, which is where the project is now. Social and city systems that turn the map into a contest. Onchain ownership and a live $MOVE economy on Base. Then a deliberate handover of governance and treasury to the community. The roadmap covers each phase and what has to be true before the next one starts.

13. Risk factors

The real risks are movement verification against spoofing, economic balance, smart-contract security, adoption density, and a regulatory landscape that keeps moving. The most immediate one is narrower than any of those: a single key currently controls every deployed contract. The full treatment is in Risk factors.
