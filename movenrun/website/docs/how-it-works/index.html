How it works

Cover ground, claim the cells you cross, and keep them by coming back. Four verbs, and the whole game sits on top of them.

Move

Walk, run, or cycle. All three count the same. Tap once and you are moving within seconds, with distance, time, and pace kept glanceable so your attention stays on the road. Pause and resume whenever you need to. There is no minimum speed and nothing to tap mid session.

Capture

The world is divided into hexagonal cells, each about 0.74 square kilometres, using H3. Every point on Earth falls in exactly one cell, and the cell is the same for every player. Two people standing on the same corner are standing in the same cell, in Lagos or in Leeds.

Finishing a session claims the cells your route crossed. That is the moment a walk becomes a foothold.

Hold

A claimed cell does not stay yours for free. Two meters govern it. Defense drains steadily from the day you last visited. Once defense is gone, control starts draining too, which is what eventually opens the cell to somebody else. Coming back restores both, and fortifying pushes defense higher still.

The effect is that the map rewards routine. A cell on your regular route is close to untouchable. A cell you claimed once on holiday will not last the month.

How a cell moves through its states
StateWhat it means
HeldYours, defended, and stable.
At riskDefense has drained low. A visit restores it.
ContestedDefense is gone and control is draining.
DormantLeft long enough that the cell is open again.

Grow

Cells group into districts, and districts into cities. Holding a critical mass of a district earns mastery, which is the long arc of the game: the neighbourhoods you actually live in become visibly yours. Clubs pool their members' ground to compete at district and city scale.

At launch, holding also starts paying. A held cell becomes a deed in your own wallet, and other players crossing it pay you a toll. See Territory and deeds.
