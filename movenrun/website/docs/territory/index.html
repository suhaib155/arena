Territory and deeds

The world is a hexagonal map, and every cell on it is ground somebody can hold. Move to take it, come back to keep it.

What a cell is

A cell is one hexagon of the real world, roughly 0.74 square kilometres, indexed with H3. Hexagons rather than squares because every hexagon borders six others at equal distance, so adjacency behaves the same in all directions. That matters in a game about spreading out from ground you already hold.

Cells nest. A group of them makes a district, districts make a city, and one grid runs from a single street to a citywide season.

Holding and losing

Every held cell carries a defense meter and a control meter. Defense drains from the moment you stop visiting. Control only starts to drain once defense is empty, so a cell you visit even occasionally stays firmly yours. Push defense low enough and the cell is at risk; leave it long enough after that and it goes dormant and reopens.

Defending restores defense and adds a little control. Fortifying is the cheaper, narrower action that raises defense alone. Both are deterministic, so the same cell in the same state always shows the same numbers.

Districts and mastery

Districts are where holding turns into standing. Take enough of one and you earn mastery over it, which is the game's way of recognising that you are genuinely there rather than passing through. Clubs combine their members' districts to contest a city.

Deeds, at launch

A deed is what a held cell becomes when the economy goes live. One deed per cell, held in your own wallet rather than in an account we control, carrying the cell's provenance and its defensive strength with it. Deeds are transferable, so ground can change hands directly as well as by contest.

Holding a deed pays. Other players who cross your cell pay a toll, and the longer you have held the cell the better the rate you keep. That is the mechanism that makes a regular route worth something: the streets you walk every morning are also the streets other people walk. The full design, including supply, is in Onchain.

Contesting ground

Ground can be taken. A challenger stakes against a cell they want, both sides' movement over the challenge window is weighed, and the deed either holds or moves. Defense, fortification, and how long the holder has held all count toward the defence. A cell you have kept through a season is genuinely harder to take than one claimed last week.
