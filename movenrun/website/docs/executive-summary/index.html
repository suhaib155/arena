Executive summary

MovenRun turns everyday movement into territory on a shared map. The app and the verification behind it work. The onchain layer that would make that territory ownable is deployed to a test network and is not finished.

The opportunity

Billions of people move every day, and almost none of that movement leaves anything behind for the person doing it. Fitness apps measure it and forget it. The first wave of move-to-earn tokens tied it to speculation and collapsed when sentiment turned. MovenRun's bet is that ownership is a more durable reason to keep going than a payout: territory you hold, build on, and can lose to somebody else.

The product

You open MovenRun and start moving. Your route lights up a hexagonal map of the real world, and the zones you cross become yours. Hold enough of a neighbourhood and it becomes a district you have mastered. Every claim is backed by a check that you actually moved, which is the part that has to be right before anything else is worth building.

Onchain, and how far along it is

Two of the three layers work. The mobile app records, scores, and saves sessions on the device. The backend has the verification service and a Postgres store holding routes, zones, and battles, and it is the system of record for anything that leaves the phone. Wiring the app's movement through it, rather than only its sign-in, is the current piece of work. Either way, no blockchain is involved.

The third layer is deployed and idle. Eight contracts went to Base Sepolia, chain 84532, on 27 May 2026: the token, the movement oracle, the zone and gear NFTs, the vault, the challenge system, the season controller, and the DAO. Nothing is on Base mainnet. No deed has been minted on any network by anyone, and nobody has accrued yield. One account holds DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE on all eight, and that same account is the oracle operator, the keeper, and the treasury. A timelock has been written but not deployed.

The intent is that deeds and balances settle on Base rather than in a company database, and that a player never has to think about any of it. The second half of that needs an embedded wallet provider and sponsored gas. Neither exists: the provider decision is still open, and there is no paymaster or account-abstraction code in the repository. See Onchain, made invisible.

The economy

$MOVE is designed as the network's asset. Verified movement would mint it on a capped, decaying schedule; captures, upgrades, challenges, and seasons would spend it; several of those actions burn it outright. None of this has run, and no $MOVE has been minted. The design is set out in the $MOVE economy.

Community ownership

MovenRun is meant to end up controlled by the people who move on it, and it is nowhere near that. Governance today is one key. What has to happen between here and onchain proposals, a deployed timelock, and community control of the treasury is set out in Governance & the DAO.

The argument

  • Ownership holds attention longer than points. Territory somebody else can take off you is worth defending. A score that only goes up is not.
  • Verification is the hard part, so it got built first. An economy running on movement is worth exactly as much as its ability to reject movement that did not happen.
  • Reach depends on hiding the crypto. This is also the piece furthest from finished, and it gates everything downstream of it.
  • Sinks before incentives. The token design puts caps and sinks ahead of emissions, because the other order has been tried in public.

The full argument, architecture, and roadmap are in the whitepaper.
