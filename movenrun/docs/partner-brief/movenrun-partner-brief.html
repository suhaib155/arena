Section 01

MovenRun in one page

If you had ninety seconds to explain MovenRun to a stranger, this is what you would say.

The plain version

MovenRun is a mobile game you play by walking, running, or cycling in the real world. We divide the entire planet into small hexagon-shaped tiles — think of a honeycomb laid over a map. When you move through a tile, you capture it. Hold a tile and it becomes yours: a piece of digital property called a Zone Deed that is recorded permanently and independently of our company, so nobody — including us — can take it away. Capture enough neighbouring tiles and you own a district. Team up with a club and you fight for a city.

The four things that make it different

1 · You own progress, you don't rent it

In a fitness app your streak is a number in someone's database. If the company shuts down, it is gone. In MovenRun your territory is recorded on a public ledger under your own keys. It survives us.

2 · Every claim is proven

Territory is only valuable if it is hard to get. Every session is checked for physical plausibility — realistic speed, sane GPS accuracy, continuous motion — before it can claim anything. Faking movement is rejected.

3 · The crypto is invisible

No seed phrases, no wallet setup, no buying a coin before you can start. You tap once and you are moving. The blockchain is doing real work underneath, and a normal user never sees a single piece of it.

4 · The economy is built to last

Previous "move-to-earn" apps printed tokens with nothing to spend them on and collapsed. We designed the ways money leaves the system before the ways it enters. That order is deliberate.

Section 02

The problem we are solving

Billions of people move every single day. Almost none of that movement builds anything for the person doing it. Three previous attempts got close and each failed in a specific, fixable way.

Failure one — fitness apps forget you

Strava, Google Fit, Apple Fitness and the rest are excellent at measuring. You walk five kilometres, they show you a number, and tomorrow that number is history. Nothing accumulates into something you hold. Your three-year streak is a graph, not an asset. And crucially, the moment you stop paying or the company changes direction, all of it is theirs to switch off.

Failure two — location platforms sell you

The other major use of movement data is advertising. Platforms collect where you go, build a profile, and sell access to it. The mover generates all the value and captures none of it. This is the model MovenRun most deliberately rejects: we are not a data broker, we do not sell location profiles, and our verification system is designed to keep proofs of movement rather than permanent trails of where you have been.

Failure three — the first "move-to-earn" wave collapsed

In 2022 a category of apps promised to pay you in cryptocurrency for walking. They grew explosively and then unravelled. The reason is simple and worth understanding properly, because avoiding it is the core of our economic design:

The difference in one picture: the old model gave people a token and no reason to keep it. Ours makes the token the thing you spend to defend what you own.

The insight underneath all three

Ownership is the most durable form of progress. A score resets. A streak breaks. A token price falls. But property you hold, that you have defended, that has a history attached to it and neighbours you fought for — that keeps meaning something regardless of what any market does. So we made the map itself the asset, and made every claim on it require proof of real effort.

Section 03

The core loop: Move → Capture → Own → Grow

Every feature we build has to serve these four verbs. If a proposed feature does not advance one of them, it is out of scope. This is our single most important internal rule.

The MovenRun core loop. It is closed on purpose: growth gives you a reason to move again tomorrow.

Move

You open the app and start a session. Walking, running, and cycling are treated as equally valid — no minimum speed, no mode that scores "better". A twenty-minute walk around your block is a first-class move here. That is a deliberate decision, not a compromise: a network that only rewards athletes never changes how ordinary people actually live.

Capture

Your route crosses hexagonal tiles on a global map, and finishing the session claims them. This is the moment a walk stops being exercise and becomes a foothold — the tiles light up along the line you actually walked.

Own

Captured zones become Zone Deeds: records of ownership held under your own keys rather than in our database. A deed is yours to keep, strengthen, defend when challenged, and transfer. This is the difference between MovenRun and every scoreboard ever built — the map remembers you because you genuinely own part of it.

Grow

Ownership compounds. Zones group into districts; districts build club and city standing; actively-held territory earns in-game yield; and holding a stake gives you a vote in how the network runs. A year of morning walks becomes a portfolio of territory and a real voice in the game's direction.

Why this matters commercially

Consistency beats intensity in our scoring. That is a health-first choice, and it is also the right retention design: the player who walks twenty minutes a day for a year is worth vastly more to the network than the athlete who runs a marathon once and disappears.

Section 04

What using the app actually feels like

A walkthrough of one real session, from the moment someone downloads the app to the moment they own a piece of their neighbourhood. No jargon in this section at all.

One complete session, start to finish. Notice what is absent from every screen: any mention of a blockchain, a wallet, or a token.

Step by step, in words

  1. You download the app and tap once. An account is created for you, and behind the scenes so is a secure wallet — but you are never shown a seed phrase or asked to install anything. You are moving within seconds.
  2. You pick a mode and press start. Walk, run, or cycle. The map shows the hexagons around you: grey means unclaimed, green means you already hold it.
  3. You go for your walk. The route draws itself live. Distance, time, and pace are shown large and simple, so your attention stays on the pavement rather than the screen. You can pause and resume freely — nothing punishes stopping.
  4. You finish and get a summary. Distance, active time, zones explored, XP earned, streak maintained. Then the satisfying part: the hexagons you crossed animate into your colour. Six new zones are yours.
  5. Over the following weeks it compounds. You return to the same routes and your hold strengthens. Neighbouring zones connect into a district. You join a club and your captures start counting toward a shared total. Somebody challenges one of your zones and you have to go out and defend it.
The design principle behind all of it

Nothing in the live session requires interaction, no mechanic rewards speed over safety, and no information depends on animation or colour alone. It is designed to be usable by someone with reduced mobility, poor eyesight, or an old phone — because that is most of the world.

Section 05

Crypto, explained from zero

You do not need to like crypto, invest in it, or find it interesting to be excellent at this job. You do need to be able to explain five ideas clearly. Here they are, each with an everyday analogy.

1 · A blockchain is a shared record book that nobody owns

Imagine a ledger in the middle of a village square. Anyone can write in it, everyone can read it, and once something is written it cannot be erased or edited — you can only add a new line. Thousands of independent people keep identical copies, so if one person tries to cheat their copy, every other copy disagrees and the cheat is rejected.

That is a blockchain. It is not a currency, a company, or a website. It is a way of keeping a record that no single party controls. Everything else in crypto is built on top of that one property.

Why we care

Because a record nobody owns is a record we cannot revoke. When we say a player genuinely owns their territory, this is the mechanism that makes it true rather than a marketing line. If MovenRun the company disappeared tomorrow, the deeds would still exist and still belong to the people who earned them.

2 · A wallet is a key, not a bank account

The word "wallet" is a poor name and causes most of the confusion. A crypto wallet does not hold anything. It holds a private key — a very long secret number that proves you are you. The ledger says "this territory belongs to key #4821…"; whoever can produce that key controls that territory.

Traditionally, users are made to write down twelve random words (a "seed phrase") that can regenerate their key, and told that if they lose those words their assets are gone forever. This single step is where the overwhelming majority of normal people abandon crypto apps — and rightly so.

What we do instead: embedded wallets

MovenRun creates the wallet for the player automatically at sign-up and secures it for them, the same way an email provider secures their inbox. There is no seed phrase to write down and nothing to install. The ownership is completely real and completely theirs — it just does not get in the way. A player who later becomes crypto-savvy can export that wallet and take full control at any time. This is the single most important product decision in MovenRun.

3 · A token is a unit that the ledger counts

If the shared record book can track who owns what, it can track quantities too. A token is simply a countable balance recorded on that ledger. Our token is called $MOVE. Inside MovenRun it works like the currency of the game: you earn it by moving, and you spend it to claim premium zones, enter challenges, and strengthen what you hold.

The important distinction: some tokens are designed as investments, and some are designed as fuel for a system. $MOVE is designed as fuel. Section 9 covers this honestly, including the parts we deliberately constrain.

4 · An NFT is a deed, not a picture

NFT stands for "non-fungible token", which is a terrible name for a simple idea. Fungible means interchangeable — any ten-rupee note is as good as any other. Non-fungible means each one is distinct and identifiable, like the title deed to a specific house.

NFTs became famous through cartoon profile pictures, and that association is unhelpful. The underlying technology is just a way of recording "this specific unique thing belongs to this specific person" on a ledger nobody controls. Land registries, ticketing, and certificates are the natural uses.

Ours is called a Zone Deed

Each hexagon on our world map has a permanent unique identifier. A Zone Deed is an NFT whose identifier is that hexagon. So the deed is not a picture of a place — the deed is the place, as far as the game is concerned. It carries who holds it, the history of how it changed hands, how strongly it is defended, and the yield it has earned.

5 · Gas is the postage stamp, and we pay it

Writing to a public ledger costs a small fee, called gas, which pays the network of computers that maintain it. Normally the user must hold some cryptocurrency to pay it — meaning a new player would have to buy a coin before they could claim their first zone. That is an absurd barrier for a walking app.

We use a technology called account abstraction to sponsor those fees on the player's behalf. The player presses "claim" and it just works. We absorb a tiny cost per transaction; on Base that cost is a fraction of a cent, which is precisely why we chose Base.

How to talk about this externally

Some people have strong negative associations with crypto, often for good reasons — scams, speculation, and collapsed schemes. Do not argue with that. The honest and disarming answer is: "You're right that a lot of it has been rubbish. We use the technology for one narrow thing — making sure the territory you earn actually belongs to you and can't be taken away. You never have to buy anything, and you'll never see a wallet." That sentence converts sceptics better than any amount of enthusiasm.

Section 06

What is Base, and why we chose it

Base is the specific blockchain MovenRun is built on. Understanding what it is — and why it is the right choice — matters, because we are applying to its accelerator programme.

The problem Base exists to solve

Ethereum is the largest and most trusted programmable blockchain, but it has a well-known weakness: it processes a limited number of transactions at a time, so when it gets busy, fees rise. At peak, one simple action could cost several dollars. For a banking application that might be acceptable. For a game where someone claims six hexagons after a morning walk, it is unworkable.

What a Layer 2 does

A "Layer 2" is a faster, cheaper network that sits on top of Ethereum and periodically writes a compressed summary of its activity back down to it. You keep Ethereum's security guarantees, but transactions are fast and cost a tiny fraction of a cent.

Why the stack matters: our users get speed and zero fees; our ownership records inherit Ethereum's security.

Section 07

How MovenRun is built: three layers

The whole system is three layers stacked on each other. If you understand this diagram you understand our architecture, and you can hold your own in any technical conversation about us.

Movement flows up; ownership settles down. The chain never takes movement on faith — it acts only on verified proofs.

Why hexagons, and not squares

We use an open standard called H3 — originally built by Uber — which tiles the entire planet into hexagons. Every point on Earth belongs to exactly one hexagon with a permanent identifier, so two players standing on the same corner are always in the same zone, anywhere in the world. We use size level 8: about 0.74 km² per tile, roughly a small neighbourhood — big enough to feel meaningful, small enough that a single walk captures several.

Even neighbours

Every hexagon touches six others at an equal distance. With squares, diagonal neighbours are further away than edge neighbours, which makes rules about spreading and defending territory messy and unfair.

Fits how people move

Real routes curve. Hexagons approximate the area around a curving path far more naturally than a square grid does.

Nests cleanly

Small hexes roll up into bigger ones, which is exactly how zones become districts and districts become cities — one grid for the whole game.

Section 10

Proof of movement: how we stop cheating

If someone can fake a walk, they can mint territory and tokens from their sofa, and the entire economy is worthless. This is the hardest technical problem in the product, and everything else depends on getting it right.

Nothing reaches the blockchain unless it has passed every check. The contracts are built to trust the proof, never the phone.

What each defence actually does

  • Speed gate. Consecutive GPS points are checked against the time between them. Anything implying more than roughly 80 km/h is thrown out — that catches the simplest cheat, which is driving.
  • Accuracy gate. Phones report how confident they are in each location fix. A session where too many points are vague gets rejected, because vague points are how spoofing tools hide.
  • Duration and continuity gates. Sessions longer than 24 hours, or containing teleport-shaped jumps, are rejected outright.
  • Route fingerprinting. The whole route is compressed into one unique fingerprint. Once that fingerprint has been used to claim something, it can never be used again — so you cannot record one good walk and submit it a hundred times.
  • The oracle signature. Our verification service cryptographically signs approved routes. The contracts on Base will only act on a proof carrying that signature, so no one can submit a fake claim directly to the blockchain, bypassing our checks.
  • Economic caps. Even a perfect cheat is bounded: there is a hard daily limit per account, and a zone requires a minimum number of distinct movers over 90 days before it becomes mintable at all. One person cannot manufacture a valuable district alone.
Honest framing

No GPS system on earth is perfectly spoof-proof, and we do not claim to be. What we claim is layered defence: several independent checks, each of which a cheater must defeat simultaneously, plus economic caps that make the effort not worth the reward. Verification is treated as a permanently ongoing investment rather than a solved problem, and it hardens as the network grows. If someone challenges you on this, that honesty is the strongest available answer.

Why the order is this way round

Notice something unusual: the blockchain work is largely done, but it is scheduled to go live third. That inversion is intentional and it is the most defensible thing about our plan.

  • A territory game needs neighbours. Owning a hexagon means nothing if nobody will ever contest it. So density has to come before ownership, which means the free, wallet-free experience has to work first.
  • An economy needs sound inputs. Switching on tradeable rewards before verification is genuinely hard to beat is how the previous generation destroyed itself. We would rather be late than exploited.

Ship the live territory map and prove the capture loop feels good. Reach real density in a first city. Switch on the embedded wallet and put deeds in players' hands on testnet. Sign the first sponsor. Only then take the economy to mainnet — with the verification and density evidence to justify it.

Section 13

How we get users

A territory game has an unusual growth property: it is better when your neighbours play, so it spreads geographically rather than randomly. That single fact shapes the entire go-to-market plan.

The core insight: density, not downloads

A hundred thousand users spread thinly across the world is a worse product than five thousand users concentrated in one city. Contested zones, rival clubs, and district wars all require neighbours. So our target metric is not total installs — it is how many active players share a city. Every growth decision follows from that.

Growth compounds locally. This is why we launch city by city rather than everywhere at once.

Seven channels, in the order we intend to use them

1 · Launch one city at a time

Pick a single city, concentrate everything on it, and do not move on until the map there is genuinely contested. A city with visible rivalry becomes its own advertisement — and gives us a repeatable playbook, a case study, and real density numbers to show partners. Diluting effort across ten cities gets you ten dead maps.

2 · Running clubs, walking groups and gyms

These groups already exist, already meet on a schedule, and are already competitive. They are the highest-leverage acquisition channel in the product because they arrive pre-formed: one club captain converts thirty players in one conversation, and thirty players in one neighbourhood is instant density. Clubs, crew missions and shared territory are built specifically to serve them.

3 · Zero-friction onboarding as a growth lever

This is not just a UX nicety — it is the difference between a 3% and a 40% conversion rate from install to first session. Every competing on-chain app loses most of its funnel at "write down these twelve words". We do not have that step at all. When someone shares MovenRun with a friend who has never used crypto, that friend can actually get in.

4 · The Base ecosystem

Base is actively looking for consumer applications that bring non-crypto users on-chain — that is precisely what Base Batches exists to fund. Being a visible flagship consumer app on Base gives us ecosystem promotion, developer community reach, and credibility with partners who already trust Coinbase. This is a distribution channel most consumer apps simply do not have access to.

5 · Local businesses and sponsored zones

A café can sponsor the zone it sits in. A gym can host an event zone. A council can sponsor a park trail. This is the same idea as a shop paying for a map pin, except players actively want to visit because it advances their game. It is our clearest early revenue line and a growth channel, because sponsors promote to their own customers. It also gives us a reason to be welcomed into a city rather than merely present in it.

6 · Territory is inherently shareable

A map of your neighbourhood shaded in your colour is a far better social object than "I walked 5 km". It is visual, personal, and immediately provokes the reaction we want: "that's my street — why is it yours?" Share cards are built into the session flow for exactly this reason.

7 · Seasons that reset the pressure

Ninety-day seasons give lapsed players a natural reason to return and new players a fair entry point where the map is not already permanently owned by veterans. Recurring competition is what keeps a network alive after the launch spike fades.

Section 14

Why us, why now

The idea of turning movement into a map game is not secret. The reason it has not been done properly is that it requires three hard things to be true at the same time — and only recently have all three become possible.

Now is possible

Only in the last couple of years have embedded wallets and sponsored fees matured enough to hide crypto completely. Two years ago this product would have required every user to buy a token before their first walk. That is what changed.

Now is affordable

Fees on Base are a fraction of a cent. A game generating thousands of small on-chain actions per day is economically viable for the first time. On the base layer it never was.

Now is credible

The move-to-earn collapse taught the whole market what not to do — and left a large audience that liked the idea and lost trust in the execution. A version built with discipline lands into a market that already understands the pitch.

What we bring that a well-funded competitor would not

  • We built the hard part first. Most teams build the app and bolt on the blockchain later, discovering too late that verification and economics do not work. We deployed and tested the contract suite, the oracle and the verification pipeline before asking anyone for money — which means our economic design has been pressure-tested against real code rather than a spreadsheet.
  • We have a written guardrail we actually enforce. Our own internal rules block a tradeable economy until verification, density and demand are all proven. That is written into the canonical scope document that governs every decision. Discipline is a competitive advantage in a category that died of the lack of it.
  • We designed for the non-crypto user from the first line of code. Not as a later "onboarding improvement" but as the founding constraint. The consequence shows everywhere: no wallet in the core loop, game words instead of crypto words, and the whole thing playable for free.
  • We are health-first, and mean it. A 20-minute walk is a first-class move. There is no minimum speed and nothing that rewards risky behaviour. That decision costs us the "hardcore athlete" audience and buys us everyone else — which is a far larger market and a far more retentive one.
  • We are documented to an unusual standard. A public whitepaper, a full technical documentation set, a threat model, a security checklist, twelve architecture decision records, and a contracts audit document. This matters because it means a new partner, an investor, or an auditor can verify our claims rather than trust them.

Section 15

Risks, stated honestly

You will be asked about these. Answering them openly is far more persuasive than deflecting — and as a partner you need to know what could actually go wrong.

RiskThe honest problemHow we address it
Movement spoofing Location can be faked with widely available tools. An economy built on movement must resist people who never leave their sofa. Layered defence — plausibility checks, deterministic route fingerprints, replay blocking, oracle attestation, hard daily caps, and a minimum number of distinct movers before a zone becomes valuable. Treated as permanently ongoing work, never "solved".
Economic balance Movement economies have collapsed when incentives outran real utility. Ours could too if we got the parameters wrong. Utility-first design: capped and decaying issuance, sinks decided before incentives, and a hard gate blocking any tradeable economy until verification and density are proven. Parameters are adjustable through governance rather than fixed forever.
Smart-contract security On-chain code is unforgiving. A bug can be irreversible and expensive. Standards-based contracts rather than reinvented primitives, narrowly-scoped roles with no single all-powerful key, timelocked governance, a documented threat model, and defence in depth. A full external audit is a prerequisite for mainnet.
Adoption density A territory game with no neighbours is a lonely map. This is our biggest commercial risk, not a technical one. Frictionless onboarding to reach beyond crypto natives, a game that is still rewarding solo, and a deliberate city-by-city rollout that builds density before it is needed rather than hoping for it.
Regulation Rules for digital assets differ by country and keep changing. Some jurisdictions may restrict parts of the model. A design centred on utility and ownership rather than financial return, no promises of income anywhere in our materials, transparent on-chain governance, and a phased rollout that can adapt market by market.
Location privacy Movement data is among the most sensitive data that exists. Mishandling it would be both a harm and an existential reputational risk. Proofs rather than permanent trails, data minimisation by design, no third-party ad tracking, no selling of location profiles, no exposure of anyone else's precise position, and user control over their own assets and keys.
Battery and phone reliability Continuous GPS drains batteries and behaves differently across hundreds of Android devices. An engineering priority for Phase 1: sampling tuned for battery, graceful handling of GPS gaps, and sessions that survive interruption. This is a known cost of the category, and it must be solved before scale.
Crypto's reputation Many people have a negative association with the word, often justified. It can cost us mainstream users before they try anything. We lead with movement, health and territory. Crypto is never the pitch — it is the mechanism, and it stays invisible. Our public language avoids financial promises entirely.
The uncomfortable one

The largest risk is not technical at all — it is that we build an excellent product and cannot reach density in a first city. Everything in Section 13 exists to attack that specific risk, and it is the one we will be measured on first. If you take ownership of one problem in this business, that is the one worth taking.

A fast, cheap network built on top of Ethereum that periodically settles down to it. Base is one.

Non-tradeable in-app credit earned before the real economy launches. Deliberately not a payout.

The real blockchain network where assets have real value — as opposed to a test network.

To create something new on the blockchain: a token, or a deed for a zone.
$MOVE
Our token. Minted by verified movement, spent on claiming, upgrading, challenging and staking.

A unique, individually identifiable record of ownership. Ours are Zone Deeds — think title deed, not cartoon.

The service that bridges the real world and the blockchain by signing off on verified routes.

The secret that proves ownership of on-chain assets. Whoever holds it controls them.

A 90-day competitive cycle with its own objectives, standings and settlement.

Holding your own private key, so no company can touch your assets. Optional in MovenRun.

A program that lives on the blockchain and runs exactly as written, the same way for everyone.

Locking tokens for a period to back the network and gain a voice in governance.

A practical onboarding path. The goal by the end of week two is that you can explain MovenRun to a sceptical stranger without notes, and have an opinion about what we should do next.
