Section 1

Start from what already works

INTVL is a running app that reached the top fifteen in health & fitness with a territory feature called Terra. Its mechanic is tested at scale. We take it as the foundation and build ownership on top.

What INTVL does — and we adopt

MechanicHow it works
Loop to claimRun or ride a closed loop and the whole area inside it becomes yours. Not tile-by-tile stepping — enclosure. Bigger, smarter routes take more map.
Cut through to stealTerritory stays yours until someone else runs those streets. No permission, no formal challenge — running through simply takes it.
Re-run to defendGo back over ground you hold to keep it. Defence is the same action as exercise.
Route plannerPreview a route before you go: see whose land you would take and how much new ground you would gain. This turns a run into a plan.
Layered leaderboardsSuburb, city, country, world. Most people chase their own neighbourhood, not the globe.
Area-weighted prize drawsEvery km² held is one entry in a monthly draw for real prizes. Rewards without printing anything.
Tactics over speedDeliberately does not favour elite runners. A clever 3 km loop beats a fast 10 km line.
Why we start here rather than inventing

This loop has been tested on real runners and it worked — it is fun before it is financial, which is the one thing every failed move-to-earn game got backwards. Our job is not to redesign the game. It is to add ownership, tolls and a token economy without breaking what already works.

What we add on top

Ownership that can be defended

In INTVL, anyone can take your land by running through it. That is exciting but nothing is ever really yours. We add minting: turn ground into a deed and it can no longer be taken by a casual pass — only by winning a contest.

Land that pays you

Every runner crossing your minted land pays you a 2% toll. Your territory stops being a score and starts being a small business — busy ground earns, empty ground does not.

Section 2

Attack and defence

Two rules, depending on whether the land has been minted. Unminted land is fast and brutal. Minted land is property, and taking it is a proper fight.

Minting is the moment ground stops being a scoreboard and becomes something worth defending.

The seven-day siege, step by step

StepCostWhat happens
Declare30 tokensYou name the deed you want. The owner is notified. A seven-day window opens.
CompetefreeBoth sides record movement inside that ground. Counted once per person per day, so it is consistency not marathons.
Fortify28 each, max 3The owner can add +20% to their score, three times. Burned whether they win or lose.
RallyfreeClub mates who run the ground add to whichever side they support. One person, one contribution per day.
Settle—Higher score takes the deed. It transfers automatically — the loser cannot refuse. The loser's stake is burned.
Cool off—A beaten attacker cannot return for 30 days. A defender who just survived gets 14 days of peace.
Why a single player can still win

Scoring counts distinct people per day, not total distance — but a determined solo runner who shows up every day for seven days beats a club of thirty who each turn up once. Nobody is locked out by not having friends, and nobody wins by buying anything: fortification helps, but it cannot beat a large gap in actual movement.

Section 3

The toll

Cross someone's minted land and 2% of what you earned there goes to them. Cross ten people's land and you still pay about 2% in total — it cannot pile up.

You pay 2% of what you earned inside each zone — not 2% of your whole run.
So the toll is bounded by how far you went, never by how many owners you passed.
The runner barely notices. The owner of a busy path collects from hundreds of runners a week.

Three things the toll fixes at once

It makes location matter — a deed on a popular path is worth far more than one in a dead cul-de-sac, so choosing ground becomes a real decision. It rewards owners without printing anything — every token an owner collects came from a runner, not from new supply. And it makes owners want more runners, which is exactly the incentive a growing network needs.

Your own land is free to cross. Unminted ground charges nothing — another reason minting is worth paying for.

Section 5

The day your earnings become real

Every player earns the same token. Whether it can be sold depends on one thing: have you minted your land?

Nothing you already earned is retroactively unlocked — that is what stops anyone farming free accounts and cashing out later.

What a free player can already sell

Being unminted does not mean earning nothing tradable. Three routes exist from day one, and none of them mints a single new token:

RouteSellable?Where the tokens come from
Staking your locked balanceThe yield isStakers share the toll pool. Your principal stays locked for ever; what it pays you is liquid.
Sponsor challengesYesSponsors deposit existing tokens. Win one as a free player and it is yours to sell.
Monthly prize drawsYesEvery km² you hold is one entry. Prizes are real goods or sponsor-funded tokens.
Everyday movement rewardsNoNewly minted, so locked. This is the supply we protect.
The rule underneath all of it

Newly minted tokens are locked. Tokens that already existed are free to move. That single line explains every case: movement rewards are new, so they lock; toll income, staking yield, sponsor prizes and draw winnings are all transfers of tokens that already exist, so they don't. A free player can earn real, sellable value from day one — they just cannot mint it out of thin air.

Section 7

What stops it being gamed

Adding tolls and combat creates new ways to cheat. Each has a specific answer.

The attackWhy it fails
Run laps of my own land to farm tollsYou never pay a toll on your own ground, so there is nothing to collect. Tolls only come from other people.
Two accounts paying each other tollsA toll is 2% of a reward that was already capped and verified. Round-tripping loses 98% each way — it is pure loss.
Buy every plot on a high streetThe 900/day account cap. Past about one good plot, more land adds no income at all.
Club of thirty takes every deedContests count distinct people per day with sharply diminishing returns, and clubs face a citywide deed limit.
Farm free accounts, mint later, cash outMinting never unlocks old earnings. Everything before the mint stays staked for ever.
Spoof GPS to claim loopsSpeed, accuracy and continuity checks; each route usable once; a signature required before anything is created.
Endless attacks to grief one ownerA beaten attacker is locked out of that deed for 30 days, and each attempt costs a stake that burns.
Marathon farmingRewards flatten after 10 km and are capped at 4.0 Effort per person per day.
The one I would watch

Deed sniping. If minting is cheap and popular ground is obvious, experienced players could mint the best paths before anyone else understands the game. Two guards: land must be held for a period before it can be minted, and minting requires the ground to have real crossing traffic already. This needs testing in the first city — it is the likeliest thing to need a parameter change.

The three questions you will be asked

"Why would I mint if my land already works?"
Because unminted ground is taken the moment somebody runs through it, earns you nothing from crossers, and pays only in locked tokens. Minting makes it defensible, toll-collecting and liquid.
"If free players can't sell, why play?"
They can. Staking yield, sponsor challenges and monthly prize draws all pay in tokens that already exist, so a free player can win sellable value from day one. Only newly minted movement rewards lock — and those buy everything in the game.
"Won't a rich player just buy the map?"
They can buy tiers, but not tolls. Land income is capped at 900 a day per account, deeds can only be taken by out-moving someone for a week, and money adds nothing to a contest score. Every token they spend trying is burned.
